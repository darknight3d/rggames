Minibus Zombie Mayhem

Chapter 1: The Short-Bus Outbreak
​The yellow NeoTech USD-400 minibus was idling outside the NeoTech facility, its tailpipe puffing thin blue smoke into the morning air. Inside, Mr. Henderson, a substitute teacher whose soul had permanently clocked out long ago, rubbed his temples as a migraine formed the shape of a horseshoe. 
​In the back row sat the crew:
​Timmy, who was aggressively trying to fit a coin into a narrow air vent.
​Gary, who had spent the last twenty minutes in absolute, transfixed rapture staring at a greasy potato chip wrapper stuck to the seat.
​Angela, who was wearing her bicycle helmet backward and actively hissing at a juice box because she suspected it was plotting against her.
​"Alright listen up," Mr. Henderson sighed. "Rule number one: do not lick the radioactive containment vats. Rule number two: stay on the bus if Gary starts staring at a hubcap again". 
​Parked right outside the gleaming glass doors of Apex Synthetic Bio-Corp, nobody predicted that Dr. Aris Thorne was about to drop a flask containing experimental mutagenic compound 7B directly into the ventilation intake. The purple gas burped out of the air vents like a malfunctioning vape pen, billowing across the parking lot and turning ordinary pedestrians into a shuffling, chaotic wave of background zombies. 
​Inside the minibus, the vapor caused a functional "factory reset". Mr. Henderson slumped over the steering wheel with the vacant emptiness of a man who finally had an excuse to stop doing attendance. Angela tipped backward out of her seat and immediately became transfixed by a very interesting pebble. 

​Chapter 2: The Parking Lot Standoff
​Outside the gleaming glass facade of Apex Synthetic Bio-Corp, the yellow minibus sat completely dead, its hazard lights blinking an indifferent, rhythmic amber. The surrounding sidewalks were a scene of standard apocalyptic panic mixed with absurd confusion, but none of the background zombies paid any attention to the vehicle. 
​Inside, the apocalypse had settled into a profound stillness:
​Timmy had successfully wedged his coin into a gap where it would remain for all eternity.
​Gary had abandoned the chip wrapper entirely, having discovered that the reflection of his own thumb in the side window was the most compelling philosophical puzzle of the century.
​Angela sat rigidly with her bicycle helmet still strapped on backward, hissing intermittently at a completely innocent ballpoint pen rolling across the floorboards.
​Gary blinked slowly, turned his head past normal human limits with a loud crunch, and pressed his nose directly against the side window glass, fogging it up completely. He wasn't looking at the fleeing crowds; he was looking at a discarded, half-eaten pretzel lying on the asphalt. 
​"Ooh," Gary whispered into the empty cabin, the apocalypse officially underway. 

​Chapter 3: The First Breach
​With the half-eaten pretzel spotted on the asphalt outside, the absolute stillness inside the yellow minibus came to an abrupt, chaotic end. 
​Gary didn't open the door like a normal person; he simply pivoted his entire upper body with a sharp, unnatural snap and nudged the emergency push-out window open with his forehead.
​Angela, still wearing her bicycle helmet backward, abandoned her staring contest with the rolling ballpoint pen and scrambled over the seats, hissing furiously at a parking meter that dared stand in her path.
​Timmy, abandoning his permanent coin-wedge, let out a sharp yelp and charged headfirst down the aisle with a megaphone he had somehow swiped from the driver's seat.
​As the bus doors hissed open, the quirky squad spilled out directly into the quarantine zone. The surrounding environment was a surreal mix of high-security military banners reading "Still Human, Still Here" and bewildered citizens wandering around in business suits with glowing, dazed expressions. The misfit crew didn't cower; instead, they immediately established their strange, chaotic perimeter right on the asphalt grid, turning a routine street corner into an accidental last stand. 

​Chapter 4: The Line in the Asphalt
​Drawn by the commotion—and perhaps by the irresistible scent of the half-eaten pretzel—the first waves of suburban wanderers began drifting toward the school bus. 
​The street markings turned into makeshift lanes as the absurdity reached its peak:
​Mr. Henderson remained slumped over the wheel inside, completely checked out and serving as the ultimate immobile anchor point for the defense.
​Angela stood brave and erratic near the open bus door, wielding a makeshift pink cape and a slingshot while hissing at a corporate zombie wandering too close.
​Timmy blasted incoherent warnings through the megaphone, while Gary fixated intensely on a stray yellow sneaker right on the yellow dividing line. 
​With helicopters chopping the air overhead and the green toxic smoke rolling past the capital monuments in the distance, the makeshift defense line was officially drawn. The misfit crew wasn't trying to save the world—they were just trying to protect their turf, setting the stage for the endless lane defense chaos to begin.

Chapter 5: The Emergency Bumper Tap
​As the morning haze burned off the asphalt outside the quarantine zone, the minibus crew remained entirely undisturbed by the escalating chaos around them. 
​Mr. Henderson continued his vigil at the wheel, his forehead resting gently against the horn in a state of eternal spiritual recess.
​Timmy finally abandoned his quest with the air vent and began aggressively inspecting a stuck gum wrapper on the floorboards, treating it like an ancient artifact.
​Angela adjusted her backward bicycle helmet and issued a sharp, defiant hiss at a passing shadow, completely winning her territorial standoff with the bus floor.
​Outside, a bewildered background zombie stumbled directly into the rear bumper of the minibus with a muffled thud. Gary turned his head halfway around with a loud crunch, blinked his wide glassy eyes, and stared intently at the zombie's left shoelace. The entire apocalypse paused for twenty seconds while Gary contemplated whether the lace was edible. 

​Chapter 6: The Final Route
​With the traffic lights blinking a useless red and green in unison, the yellow minibus sat anchored in the center of the grid like a monument to bureaucratic exhaustion.
​The human survivors outside realized that trying to reason with the horde was entirely pointless. You couldn't fight a zombie apocalypse that was too distracted by dropped snacks, hubcaps, and random street graffiti to notice it was the end of the world. 
​As the sirens wailed in the distance, Mr. Henderson let out one final, low, breathy murmur that echoed through the empty cabin:
“Ooh… attendance.” 
​And with that, the minibus remained parked right where it belonged—infinitely stuck in the greatest field trip detour in human history.
